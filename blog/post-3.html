What Expert AI Training Actually Looks Like

A lot of the AI agents getting good at real work right now aren't learning from scraped text. They're learning from people — experts recording themselves doing the job, step by step, so an AI agent can watch, imitate, and eventually handle a version of that work itself. I've been doing this kind of work directly, and it's different from almost everything else people picture when they hear “AI training.”

What the work actually looks like

In the industry this is called expert demonstration data or agentic training data — real examples of skilled work that an AI agent can learn from. In practice, it breaks into three parts:

  1. Recording real, professional tasks. Not a scripted demo — the actual steps someone with real expertise takes to get a real task done, captured step by step.
  2. Writing clear task specs. Plain documentation of what's being done, where, and why — so the AI isn't just copying motions, it's learning the reasoning behind them.
  3. Quality review before anything ships. Every recording goes through metadata tagging and QC so the AI labs on the other end can trust the data they're using.

It's closer to being a subject-matter expert on a film set than it is to “doing AI stuff.” The skill that matters most is the one you already have — design, admin, finance, law, engineering, whatever your real job is.

Why it matters, and who it's for

AI agents are only as good as the examples they've seen of real work being done well. The more varied and credible that expert data is, the better agents get at handling judgment calls, not just repetitive tasks — which is exactly the gap between today's AI tools and ones that can actually be trusted with real responsibility.

That makes this a fit for anyone with genuine, provable expertise: design, business operations, admin, finance, law, engineering, and plenty of fields beyond those. You don't need an AI background. You need to be good at your actual job and willing to show your work.
